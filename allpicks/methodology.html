Exactly how every number on this board is produced, and what we refuse to produce. Generated 2026-10-14T03:47:12Z.

1. What Hotspawn gives us — and what it does not

From play.hotspawn.com/lobby and /cs2-pickems we take fixture identity only: the fixture id, the two team names, the competition, Hotspawn's tier and point value, and the UTC start and lock epochs embedded in the page's Angular TransferState payload.

Hotspawn's “% picked” figures are never used as a probability. They are a crowd poll of the site's own users, sampled at an unknown size, and the brief for this project excludes them explicitly. The same rule applies to HLTV's “Pick a winner” vote, which is also a crowd poll (for example HLTV shows 87.5%/12.5% on fixture 1079624; that number appears nowhere in our output).

2. Coverage: 89 fixtures, 28 open

Hotspawn's own tier filter reports 88–89 CS2 fixtures while its page header says “68 matches this week”. We publish what the payload actually contains — 89 CS2 rows — and record the disagreement as a flag rather than claiming the header proves coverage. Of those, 28 have a lock time still in the future and are pickable; 2 are locked/started; the remainder are June–July 2026 fixtures Hotspawn still carries.

3. Outside market lines

A bookmaker price is converted to a two-way probability by removing the overround:

p(A) = (1/o_A) / (1/o_A + 1/o_B)    overround = (1/o_A + 1/o_B − 1) × 100

  • Tier A — a primary bookmaker's own public price feed, read by CI during the run (Pinnacle's public guest moneyline endpoint, the payload its CS2 matchups page loads). Decimal odds are converted from the American prices in that response.
  • Tier B — a book price we could not re-read during this build. Two kinds, labelled apart on every row: a direct read of a primary book’s own price feed at an earlier timestamp (Pinnacle’s public guest API, read 2026-10-14T01:48Z by session arena/8944bb84), and a named aggregator’s report of a book price (EGamersWorld reporting bc.game). We print the book, the exact URL carrying those numbers and the read time, and we state plainly that the price was not re-read this cycle.
  • Citation integrity: the book label, the odds and the source URL on a row always come from the same read. Eight fixtures carry two independent reads upstream whose numbers differ — for example Pinnacle 2.700/1.410 against EGamersWorld’s bc.game 2.450/1.480 on fixture 1079546. The primary read wins and the other is printed as a labelled cross-check, never mixed into one row.
  • Rejected: EGamersWorld prints 1.80/1.80 when it has no price. Identical prices on both sides carry zero information, so those rows are rejected and flagged, never de-vigged into a fake 50/50.
  • Orientation: aggregators do not always list the teams in Hotspawn's order. Odds are mapped by team name, not by column position, and every swap is recorded in data/5bcfd1b5/market_reads.csv.

4. Our own line

p_map(A) = 1 / (1 + 10^(-k · (pts_A − pts_B) / 400))

k = 0.463 — cs2 calibrate.py (MLE on Valve's published match results), fitted on 14188 of Valve's own published match results. This constant is inherited from main's calibrated model (cs2/data/summary.json) and is read at build time, not hard-coded.

Series transform from a single-map probability: BO1 identity; BO3 p²(3-2p); BO5 p³(10-15p+6p²). Both transforms are strictly increasing on [0,1] and fix 0.5, so the favourite side is format-invariant. Where the format is unverified we publish the single-map estimate and label it as such; the pick is still sound.

Rating input: official Valve Regional Standings points for the period in force at the match date — the latest Valve publication on or before that date. If the team is absent there, we use the earliest later official period containing that exact name and flag the row as anachronistic. This is the single biggest reason the previous boards had 25 blank picks: they read only the 2026-10-05 file, but 23 of the blank fixtures were played in June and July 2026.

5. Pick hierarchy

The first tier with a usable input decides the pick. Tiers are never averaged.

  • A — primary book moneyline, de-vigged.
  • B — named secondary book moneyline, de-vigged.
  • C — our own line, both teams uniquely ranked in the official Valve standings in force at the match date.
  • D — our own line where a rating needed a roster-verified alias, a pinned duplicate-name row, or a later Valve period. Same arithmetic, weaker provenance, labelled.
  • E — directional only: one side officially ranked, the other confirmed unranked in every Valve period and on HLTV. A pick is published; the probability is deliberately blank because no official number exists for the unranked side.
  • F — no pick: no official rating for both sides and no market price. Blank and flagged.

6. Duplicate team names in Valve's own file

Valve's standings legitimately contain more than one row with the same team name (an active five-man roster and an older or partial one). We handle this in two ways and never guess:

  • If a roster check pins one row — inherited from data/8944bb84/ratings.csv and re-verified line by line against Valve's file before use (94 accepted, 0 rejected) — that row is used and the pin is cited.
  • Otherwise every same-name row stays admissible and we evaluate all combinations. If they all agree on the favourite, the pick is published at the conservative bound of the probability range and the range is printed. If they disagree on the favourite, no probability and no pick are published at all.

7. What we never do

  • No invented odds, timestamps, ratings, rosters or formats.
  • No 50/50 fallback, no averaging of market and model, no crowd poll as a probability.
  • No mixing of HLTV's VRS: #N badge numbers with Valve's published standings. They are different snapshots: HLTV shows HEROIC as “VRS #22” while Valve's 2026-10-05 file ranks HEROIC #15 (1550 pts). We use Valve's file and cite the exact line.
  • Unverifiable means blank plus a flag on Flagged for review.

8. Limits

Valve's standings are published roughly monthly, so a rating can be weeks older than the match and cannot reflect a last-minute roster change. A points logistic is a coarse model: it knows nothing about map pool, form, travel or a team's motivation in a dead rubber. Aggregator prices can drift within the hour, and we print the read time so you can judge the age yourself. Locked and expired fixtures cannot be picked in Hotspawn's game; their rows are archival and are labelled as such. Nothing here is betting advice.
