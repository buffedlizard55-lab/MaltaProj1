How every number here is produced

  1. Fixtures. The 4 public pick’em pages we crawl are Angular SSR; the <script id="ng-state"> TransferState carries the whole fixture list, not only the rendered cards, so nothing depends on how many cards the page chose to paint. Each row gives scheduled_start_time and lock_time as epoch milliseconds — converted to UTC and published in full. A fixture is pickable while its lock time is in the future.
  2. External line. Pinnacle’s public guest responses (the payload its own CS2 matchups page loads) give the whole-match moneyline; EGamersWorld match pages give named books’ aggregate prices. Both are converted to decimal, de-vigged multiplicatively, and averaged across the books when more than one quotes the fixture. Pinnacle, when it lists the fixture, is the headline because it sets the market other books follow.
  3. Joining a price to a fixture. Only exact normalised team-pair equality plus a start time within 6 h, and the pairing must be unique. Group stages where the same two teams play twice are resolved by kickoff time; anything ambiguous is refused and flagged.
  4. Our own line. Valve Regional Standings points through P(A) = 1/(1+10^(−k·Δ/400)). k is re-fitted by maximum likelihood on the match results Valve publishes in the same repository (14188 matches over 44 snapshot windows), because Valve’s README states the raw curve over-confidently separates strong and weak teams. Out-of-sample it beats the raw curve: log loss 0.6915 → 0.6509, Brier 0.241 → 0.2296. The curve is fitted on match results, so it is a match-level probability; no series transform is layered on top of it (format mixtures are already inside the data the slope was fitted on).
  5. The pick. The market favourite when a verified line exists, otherwise our own line’s favourite, labelled as such on the row. When the two disagree on direction the row carries flag A-DIVERGE and both numbers stay visible.
  6. Blanks. No price and no official rating for both teams → no probability, no pick, and a flag saying what was missing. Nothing is filled in from a neighbouring round, an "average team", or Hotspawn’s own crowd percentage.
  7. Reproduce. python3 cs2/collect.py (needs internet, run by the workflow) → VRS_REPO_DIR=<clone> python3 cs2/calibrate.py → python3 cs2/model.py → python3 cs2/build.py → python3 cs2/tests.py.
