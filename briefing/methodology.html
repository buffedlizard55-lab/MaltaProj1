How a pick is chosen

1. The fixture list comes from Hotspawn, nothing else

The board reads the Angular TransferState payload embedded in play.hotspawn.com/lobby (and the CS2 and Thunderpick pick'em pages) and keeps the CS2 rows. From it we take only: the two team names, the tournament, the tier, the stage, the UTC kickoff, the UTC lock time and the permalink. Hotspawn's crowd pick percentages are dropped at parse time and are never written to disk, so they cannot reach a probability by accident.

2. Probabilities come from outside markets first

For each open fixture we look for a price, in this order, and stop at the first one we find:

  1. Pinnacle — its own public guest API (sport 12, esports), whole-match moneyline only.
  2. Bookmaker — the price bo3.gg's public API relays, with the bookmaker named and a link to the bookmaker's own match page.
  3. Polymarket — its match-winner market. A prediction market, not a bookmaker; it is always labelled, and a market with a spread above 0.06 or volume below 500 is recorded but never used.
  4. Our own line — see below, used only when no market prices the match.

A match must be identified by both its two team names and a kickoff inside ±150 minutes. A name that matches only after stripping a corporate suffix (Esports, Gaming, Team, Club, GG) is joined but flagged.

3. De-vigging

Two-way proportional: p(A) = (1/oddsA) / (1/oddsA + 1/oddsB). The bookmaker's overround is published next to every market-derived probability so the reader can see how much margin was removed. Pinnacle publishes American prices; these are converted to decimal exactly and stored as read.

4. Our own line for matches no market prices

Where neither a bookmaker nor a prediction market prices a match, we originate a line from the official Valve Regional Standings (2026_10_05):

  • map win probability p = 1 / (1 + 10^(-(ratingA - ratingB) / scale))
  • series probability from the sourced best-of: BO3 p²(3-2p), BO5 p³(10-15p+6p²)
  • scale = 2000.0 rating points per 10:1 map-odds ratio, fitted by least squares against the de-vigged market prices read in this same run (n=12, RMSE 5.45 pp, worst 12.86 pp)
This is our model, not Valve's official forecast formula. It is fitted in-sample on a single day's market prices, so it is a description of how those markets priced rating gaps that day, not a validated predictor. Where a market line exists the market wins; our line is shown beside it and any disagreement in direction is flagged on the row and listed on Flagged for Review.

5. The pick

The pick is simply the higher-probability side of the highest tier available. We do not average market and model. If the two sides price identically the pick is left blank rather than broken arbitrarily. If neither a market price nor official ratings for both teams can be sourced, the probability stays blank and the row is flagged — we never assume 50/50.

6. What is deliberately not done

  • Hotspawn crowd percentages are never an input.
  • No series format is assumed from Hotspawn (its payload leaves best_of null); the format comes only from a source that states it, otherwise the model line is published as a single-map estimate and flagged.
  • No historical price is carried into a current pick.
  • Historical or secondary aggregator reports are not used as probabilities.

7. Automation

.github/workflows/briefing-198cf31e.yml re-reads every source on a 4-hourly schedule, on push and on demand. It publishes only if collection, the render, the regression tests and a zero-broken-link check all pass — a failed run leaves the previous board live. Raw responses are archived (with HTTP status, byte count, SHA-256 and capture time) so every published number re-derives from bytes that were actually received.

Analysis only, not betting advice. Hotspawn supplies fixture identity and lock times only; its crowd pick percentages are never used.
